# Interview question bank

Ask one round at a time. Keep it to 3–5 questions per message, stay friendly, and
adapt the wording to what they've already told you. If an answer covers a later
question, skip it. After each round, give a short "Here's what I heard" summary,
ask them to correct anything, then save it to the right file.

Don't assume they sell anything, have a free resource, or want leads. Many people
just want to post consistently and be known for something.

Open with something like:

> "Before I write anything, I want to understand you, your work, and what you want
> LinkedIn to do for you. I'll ask a few short rounds of questions. Skip anything
> you don't know yet. Rough answers, links, and pasted notes all work."

---

## Round 1: You and your work → `profile.md`

1. What do you do, in a sentence or two? (Paste your website or LinkedIn "About" if that's easier.)
2. What's your background, and why do people listen to you on this topic? (Experience, results, story.)
3. What are you working on **right now**? (Projects, client work, things you're building or learning.)
4. What's coming up in the next 3–6 months? (Launches, events, a new role, hiring, a book, a busy or slow season.)
5. What do you want LinkedIn to do for you? Rank whatever applies: be known as an expert, stay visible to your network, attract clients, build a following, hire, find partners, get speaking gigs, grow an email list, or something else.

## Round 2: Who you want to reach → `profile.md`

1. Who do you most want reading your posts? (Job titles, type of person, industry, company size, location.)
2. What problems, goals, or questions are on their mind? What do they say, in their own words?
3. What does their week look like? (Recurring meetings, planning sessions, deadlines, busy seasons.) This helps tie posts to real moments, like "before your Monday team meeting."
4. Who **isn't** a fit? Anyone you'd rather not attract?
5. Anyone else worth reaching? (Referral partners, peers, recruits, media.)

## Round 3: Content preferences and rhythm → `pillars.md`

1. What topics do you know cold, and could talk about forever?
2. Which kinds of posts appeal to you? Pick any: how-to or checklists, personal stories, behind-the-scenes, opinions, lessons learned, client or project wins, industry news commentary, tips lists, document or carousel posts, short videos, polls, announcements.
3. Is there anything you **won't** post about? (Politics, family, competitors, specific clients, money.)
4. How often do you realistically want to post, on which days, and at what times? Which time zone?
5. Are there LinkedIn creators whose posts you like or dislike? What specifically about them? (This is for inspiration on format only. Never copy their voice.)

## Round 4: Offers and how often to promote (optional) → `offers.md`

Start by saying something like: *"Most of your posts will just be useful,
with an easy call to action like 'try this before your next team meeting.' No
asking for anything. Some people also want to occasionally mention something they
offer. That's completely optional."*

1. Is there anything you'd like to mention from time to time? For example, a service, a product, a free resource (guide, checklist, template, webinar), a booking or calendar link, a newsletter, an event, or a community. Or nothing at all? For each one: name, link, who it's for, and price if relevant.
2. **How often do you want a post to promote something?** Offer choices:
   - Never. Just useful posts.
   - Rarely (about once a month)
   - Sometimes (about one of every 5–6 posts)
   - At most one of every 4 posts. This is the most often the skill allows, because every promotional post must follow at least 3 value posts.
   - Only around specific dates (a launch, an event, open enrollment)
3. When you do mention something, how direct do you want it to be? A light "it's here if you want it", or a clear invitation?
4. Do you have results or testimonials you're allowed to share? Is there anything you can't say publicly (pricing, client names, compliance limits)?
5. If you have a free resource or booking link, where do people land? (Your website, an opt-in page, a calendar tool.) Is there an email follow-up? (Note it so links can be tracked. Don't build funnels unless they ask.)

Save the answer to question 2 as **Ask cadence** in `offers.md`. If they pick
"never" or have nothing to offer, write `Ask cadence: never` and move on. Every
post will use a soft CTA.

## Round 5: Voice → `voice.md`

1. Paste 3–10 things **you** wrote that feel like you: past LinkedIn posts, emails, newsletters, website copy, even text messages. More is better.
2. Pick three words for how you want to come across. (For example: warm, blunt, nerdy, calm, funny, polished, scrappy.)
3. Emojis: none, a few, or plenty? Hashtags: none, 1–3, or more?
4. Words, phrases, or styles you love? Ones you hate or never want to see?
5. First person ("I") or company voice ("we")? Does that change between your profile and your Page?

> If they have no writing samples, offer to interview them instead: ask them to
> explain one topic to you as if you were a colleague, then use their phrasing as
> the voice sample.

## Round 6: Media → `media-library.md` + `media/`

1. Do you have photos you'd like to use regularly? (Headshots, working shots, events, team, product, workspace.) Drop them into `linkedin-autopilot/media/`, or share a folder link.
2. Any brand assets? (Logo, colors, fonts, Canva templates, a brand kit.)
3. Any existing material worth reusing? (Slide decks, PDFs, videos, podcast episodes, blog posts, talks.) These become a content mine.
4. Any visual inspiration? Accounts, posts, or styles you want images to feel like.
5. Clean photos, or images with text overlays? Do you use Canva or another design tool?

List each file in `media-library.md` with a short description, the pillar it fits,
and usage notes ("only use on Page", "client approved").

## Round 7: Destinations → `profile.md`

1. Should posts go to your **personal profile**, a **company Page**, or both?
2. If there's a Page: what's its name and URL? Are you a **Super admin** or **Content admin**? (You need one of those roles to post as the Page.)
3. If both: how should they differ? (For example, profile for stories, lessons, and opinions, and Page for company news, how-tos, and announcements.)
4. Should one reshare the other?

## Round 8: Posting method → `profile.md`

1. How comfortable are you with tech? (1 = "just tell me what to click", 5 = "I'm fine with code and APIs")
2. Do you already use Zapier, Make, Buffer, Hootsuite, GoHighLevel, or another scheduler?
3. Should I post **for** you automatically, or would you rather I write and you click "schedule"?
4. Budget for tools: $0, a small monthly fee, or whatever works best?

Then recommend a method from `posting-methods.md` and walk them through setup.

---

## When the interview is done

Give them a one-screen summary:
- Who they are and who they want to reach
- What LinkedIn should do for them
- 3–5 proposed pillars and the weekly rhythm
- Ask cadence (for example "never", or "1 in 6 posts, always after 3+ value posts") and what, if anything, gets promoted
- Destinations
- Recommended posting method

Ask: "Anything wrong or missing?" Then move on to Phase 2.
