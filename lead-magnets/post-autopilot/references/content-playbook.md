# Content playbook: formats and structure (not voice)

This file covers the **shape** of a post. How it **sounds** always comes from
`voice.md`. If a format here clashes with the user's voice or preferences, the user
wins.

## Post types and when to use them

| Type | What it does | Basic structure |
|---|---|---|
| Teach / how-to / checklist | Builds authority, gets saves | Problem → full steps or checklist → result → soft CTA tied to a moment in their week |
| Story | Builds trust and connection | Moment → tension → turning point → lesson for the reader → reflection question |
| Proof / win | Shows it works | Situation → what was done → result (approved numbers only) → what the reader can borrow |
| Opinion | Starts conversation | Clear stance → reasons → nuance → question |
| Behind the scenes | Humanizes the person or brand | What you're working on → why → what you're learning |
| Curated / commentary | Stays relevant | News or another post → your take → what it means for your audience |
| Poll | Easy engagement plus audience research | A question your audience cares about, with 2–4 options |
| Document / carousel | Saves and dwell time | 6–12 slides, one idea per slide, uploaded as a PDF |
| Ask post (optional) | Mentions an offer | Useful content first → one plain mention plus link. Only after 3+ soft posts (see `ctas-and-offers.md`) |
| Video | Personality and reach | 30–90 seconds, captions on, hook in the first 3 seconds |

## Building any post

1. **Opening line(s):** LinkedIn cuts the post off after the first two or three lines on mobile. Those lines have to make the right person want to click "…more". Write several options and let the user's voice decide the style.
2. **Body:** One idea. Short paragraphs or line breaks if the user writes that way. Specific beats general: name the situation, number, mistake, or result.
3. **Close:** A single CTA. By default it's a **soft CTA**: use this before a real moment in the reader's week ("before your next Monday priorities meeting", "at your next team check-in"), reflect, or add to the discussion. Promotional "ask" CTAs follow the rules in `ctas-and-offers.md`. Never write "comment [WORD] to get it".
4. **Length:** LinkedIn allows up to 3,000 characters. Match length to the user's samples, not to a formula.
5. **Hashtags and emojis:** Use exactly what `voice.md` says.
6. **Media:** Prefer the user's own photos from `media/`. Real faces and real work usually beat stock imagery. Always write alt text.

## Weekly mix

Don't impose a ratio. Offer a starting point based on their goals, then let them
choose. Value posts with soft CTAs are always the majority. Ask posts follow
the user's ask cadence, and there are always **at least 3 soft-CTA posts before
each one**. Some examples:

- **Consistency / authority focus:** teach, story, opinion, and commentary, all with soft CTAs, and no asks at all
- **Visibility plus occasional offer:** mostly teach and story, with one ask post after every 3–5 value posts (per their cadence)
- **Around a launch or event:** problem → how-to → story (all soft) → one ask post → back to value

## Calendar planning

For each calendar row, record: date, time with time zone, destination (profile or
Page), pillar, post type, working hook, CTA type (soft/ask), the soft CTA idea or the item promoted, media
file, and status (idea → drafted → approved → scheduled → posted).

Spread pillars across the week so no two neighboring posts feel the same. Line up
any ask posts with real dates from `offers.md` (launches, events), and check the
3-soft-before-every-ask rule before showing the calendar.

## Turning raw material into posts

Users often have more content than they realize. Mine it:
- One long piece (blog, podcast, webinar, video) → 5–10 posts (each key point, a story, a quote, a stat, a myth)
- One client result → a proof post, a "mistake I see" post, and a how-to
- One FAQ from sales calls → a teach post and an opinion post
- Voice memos or notes → cleaned up into their voice, keeping their exact phrases

## Profile and Page basics (offer once)

Posting works better when the profile converts. Offer a quick review covering:
headline (who you help and how), banner, About
section, the Featured section (pin your best post or resource), custom button or link, and on
the Page: tagline, description, and CTA button.
